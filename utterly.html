Something Utterly Original 2025

----------------------------------

As a child, I would gaze into a glass marble, fascinated by the fractured light and shifting shapes within — a tiny world mirroring the outside in strange, unrecognizable ways. A world just beyond my reach.

Now, that world lies fractured, each shard resembling an unevenly cut cake. These fragments — like layers of sponge, cream, fruit, and jam — are composed of interwoven histories. The topping is a series of miniature scenes, or key moments, spanning fifty years of self-portraits.

The title, Something Utterly Original, acknowledges the paradox of contemporary artistic practice—the expectation to be radically innovative while simultaneously responding to societal pressures, ideological shifts, culture wars, and institutional demands. Rather than making this paradox my research subject, it serves as the trigger for my turn to microhistory and lived experience. Through memory and self-representation, I seek an alternative mode of engagement — one that prioritizes reflection over reaction, nuance over immediacy.

While my earlier films and digital works questioned large-scale systems — finance, media, speculative futures — Something Utterly Original uses these observations and critiques as a backdrop for microhistories. These self-portraits, drawn over the course of a four-month residency, trace moments of wonder and curiosity against the backdrop of shared Western European history.

Rather than positioning this project as a purely personal response, I see it as part of a broader artistic dialogue — one that questions how artists navigate expectation, originality, and self-representation today.
