Propaganda & Advertising as Quantum Manipulations 2025

In a quantum system, observation alters reality — just as in media, framing determines perception. The collapse of a wave function into a definite state mirrors how propaganda and advertising shape public consciousness by reducing complex realities into singular narratives. This research investigates how immersion, nonconscious cognitive processes, and multisensory engagement contribute to ideological manipulation.

McLuhan’s concept of Acoustic Space from the fifties describes an immersive, fragmented, and non-hierarchical sensory experience, which contrasts with Ocular Centrism, a linear, measurable, and controlled perspective. Similarly, in quantum physics, systems exist in a state of indeterminacy until observed. This offers a compelling parallel to how propaganda and algorithmic persuasion function: by collapsing multiple realities into controlled, simplified narratives.

Quantum entanglement — where particles remain connected regardless of distance — resonates with contemporary digital media landscapes. Algorithmic influence creates information bubbles, pulling individuals into polarized networks without a single vantage point. Likewise, wave-particle duality suggests a model for understanding media consumption: propaganda operates in an immersive, wave-like state of affect and impulse, bypassing rational reflection, much like quantum particles shifting between states depending on observation.

Drawing from Katherine Hayles' Cognitive Nonconscious, from her book Unthought, 2017 this research examines how propaganda and advertising operate below the level of rational thought. If Acoustic Space is an immersive and participatory cognitive state rather than a reflective one, it aligns with nonconscious knowledge processing — where AI, bacterial intelligence, and mycelial networks function as decentralized cognitive models. Posthumanism suggests a shift away from human-centric higher consciousness, raising the question: does this make us more susceptible to manipulation, or does it offer new modes of resistance?

Both propaganda and quantum physics manipulate perception at fundamental levels. If media functions as an observer in a quantum system, does it “collapse” complex realities into singular, consumable narratives? Immersive media environments, from advertising to psychological warfare, operate by bypassing critical reflection and placing audiences in non-reflective states. Digital surveillance and algorithmic persuasion further shape political and economic behavior, creating a media landscape where agency is increasingly decentralized and fragmented.

Speculative fiction provides an essential framework for exploring alternative cognitive spaces and resistance strategies. Authors such as Ursula K. Le Guin, Stanisław Lem, Philip K. Dick, and N.K. Jemisin present models of decentralized perception, posthuman intelligence, and immersive realities that parallel the way Acoustic Space functions in media systems. Through speculative fiction, this research not only analyzes but also imagines new epistemologies beyond the limits of ocular-centric propaganda.
